# Who Areta is for

Decided 2026-09-09 after a product audit found no target user written down anywhere.
Every screen review, copy decision, and scope call checks against this page.

## The product, in one sentence

**A coach that plans your week and rewrites it every Sunday from what actually happened —
without you logging.**

The Week and the Sunday brief are the center. Today is execution: tap what you did.
Insights are evidence inside the brief (and occasionally a story card worth sharing), not
the product. Sharing is growth. The closest real-world analog is the weekly job of a
$150–300/month human coach — look at the week, adjust the plan, say why — done in software
for the price of a streaming subscription.

Areta is **not** a personal operating system, a dashboard, a logger, a readiness score, or
a trainer marketplace. Every one of those makes the app harder to explain and none of
them makes the Sunday adjustment better.

## Marcus, 34

- Desk job. iPhone and Apple Watch for four years — years of Health history he has never
  looked at.
- Up 15 lb over two years. Used to be fitter; wants to be again.
- Tried MyFitnessPal twice. Quit both times inside three weeks because logging is a chore.
- Goes to a normal gym two or three times a week with no program. Cooks three or four
  nights; eats out the rest.
- Wants the 15 lb gone **without becoming someone who weighs chicken**.
- Would pay $8–10 a month for something that removes decisions.

Age band 28–45: the people with multi-year Health data, disposable income, and the "I
used to be fitter" motivation.

**Accessibility bar: built for Marcus, usable by Marcus's mom.** (Replaces the old
"design-savvy teens and first-time elderly" bar, which was everyone and therefore no one.)

## Who Areta is not for

- **The Whoop / Oura enthusiast.** He wants dashboards, readiness scores, and raw data.
  Areta will always feel shallow to him, and chasing him is how it becomes Whoop.
- **The founder's own situation** (surgical recovery, grad school, simultaneous goals) is a
  niche. Never bake it into core logic.

## The day-one story

*"You'll rarely type a meal. Tap the one we planned; when you eat out, tell us roughly."*

Because Areta planned the meal, the user confirms it rather than logging it. Health fills
in steps, sleep, workouts, and weight with zero effort. On day one, Marcus should be able
to say what Areta is after one screen, connect Health with one tap, answer roughly nine
tappable questions, and have tonight's dinner and tomorrow's workout on his phone.

## The week-one bet

The first Sunday brief must contain **one specific adaptation with its reason**:

> You made 3 of 3 evening sessions and 0 of 2 at 6 am, and your sleep held either way.
> That's a schedule problem, not a discipline one — so I've moved Thursday to 6 pm and
> trimmed Friday to 2,150 kcal for the lighter week. We'll see next Sunday if it held.

If the first brief says "great week, keep it up," Areta is a newsletter and the
differentiator is dead. The quality of the first Sunday is the product; everything else
is packaging.

## Why it isn't MyFitnessPal

MyFitnessPal records what you did. Fitbod prescribes what to do. Whoop scores your body.
None of them close the loop — plan, observe automatically, adjust with evidence, test the
change next week. Areta does, and it can only do it because it sees nutrition, training,
sleep, and weight at once: "you slept 40 minutes less after evening workouts, so I moved
Thursday to the morning" is a sentence a single-domain app cannot write.

## Preferences the week bends to

The week is the cadence of *adjustment*, not a rule about how people live. Remembered in
Settings, honored by the generators, never re-asked:

- Shopping and prep horizon — one, two, or three weeks at a time.
- Which nights they cook and which they eat out.
- Training only, no meal planning at all.
- Their own review day.

## Pricing shape

The full loop free for about 30 days (two Sunday briefs), then a subscription. Charge for
continuing, not for features: a free tier that withholds the weekly adjustment means free
users never experience the reason to pay.

## Scope, for now

Trainer console parked (code kept, entry points hidden). Web is the landing page, account
and privacy pages, and the trainer/admin consoles — the consumer product is the iPhone app.
